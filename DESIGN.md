# DESIGN.md
### The Creative Constitution — Kyle & Elizabeth · The Madrona, Healdsburg · 05.01.2027

> This document exists to preserve the **feeling** of this wedding before we decide what the
> website is made of. It is deliberately not a spec. There are no hex codes, no font stacks,
> no spacing scales, no component APIs, no grid definitions here. Those come later, and only
> after the feeling is locked.
>
> **Sequence we are following:** `SENSATION → VISUALS → SYSTEM`.
> We are in **SENSATION**. Guard it. Do not let the artifact arrive early and flatten the encounter.
>
> If a future decision ever contradicts this document, this document wins until we consciously
> revise it together. This is canon, not a mood board.

---

## 0. How to read this

Every section below is a **constraint on feeling**, not a menu of features. When we build,
each page, transition, and mark should be able to point back to a sentence in here and say
"this is why I exist." If it can't, it's decoration, and decoration that doesn't carry the
world is the one thing we cannot afford.

The single most useful test in this whole document, repeated here so it's impossible to miss:

> **Would someone remember this after they close the tab?**
> **Does it make the wedding world feel more real?**
> If not, cut it — no matter how technically impressive it is.

---

## 1. The world in one sentence

**Someone with extraordinary, slightly eccentric taste has spent years collecting beautiful
and strange things, planted an orange grove, and then invited everyone over for a drink.**

The website is the envelope that world arrives in. Opening it should feel like being let in
on something — warm, a little unexpected, unmistakably *theirs*.

The social register is exactly:

> "Of course this is beautiful. Come have a drink."

Never:

> "Look how expensive this is."

---

## 2. The emotional world

The site should feel **warm, tactile, expressive, eclectic, playful, layered, human,
collected, slightly unexpected, soulful, relaxed, creative — sophisticated but not precious.**

Five words sit above the rest and govern the others. When they conflict with anything, they win:

1. **TEXTURED** — you can almost feel it. Ink on toothy paper, not pixels on glass.
2. **WHIMSICAL** — wit, surprise, a raised eyebrow. Never twee, never cute.
3. **ARTFUL** — authored by a hand with a point of view. Composed, not templated.
4. **ORGANIC-Y** — grown, drawn, bent, asymmetric, alive. Not botanical wallpaper.
5. **FUN** — joy is the baseline emotion, not a garnish.

If a screen is beautiful but reads as *careful*, *neutral*, *tasteful-anonymous*, or *safe*,
it has failed all five at once.

---

## 3. California laid-back rich sophistication

This is the load-bearing idea, and the easiest one to get wrong, because most "luxury" design
vocabulary is East Coast by default. **We are the opposite of that.**

Sophistication here does **not** come from:
prep, formality, perfect symmetry, monograms, gloss, manicured perfection, restraint-as-status,
or any visible wealth signaling.

Sophistication here comes from:
**art · history · patina · beautiful materials · gardens · architecture · food · objects
collected over time · eccentric detail · California light · indoor/outdoor living ·
effortless hospitality.**

The distinction, felt:

| East Coast rich (AVOID) | California rich (THIS) |
|---|---|
| New, pristine, correct | Aged, patinated, lived-in |
| Symmetry as authority | Asymmetry as confidence |
| Monogram, crest, seal-as-status | A wax seal used for *joy*, not heraldry |
| Beige quiet-luxury | Saturated, warm, alive |
| "Appropriate" | "Interesting" |
| Wealth you can price | Taste you can't buy |
| Polished to remove the hand | Finished enough to keep the hand |

The Madrona is our north star for this exact distinction: an 1881 Aesthetic-Movement mansion
that refused the Tuscan/Provençal cosplay of every other wine-country hotel, and instead filled
itself with a real art collection, antiques and curios from lifetimes of travel, pattern,
excess, and whimsy — statement pieces as the *norm*, not the exception. Rich because it's
been *collected*, not because it's been *bought*.

---

## 4. One world, many atmospheres — different atmospheres, same DNA

This is one of the most important ideas in the entire wedding, and the site must embody it.

The wedding does not repeat one aesthetic everywhere. Guests move through genuinely **distinct
atmospheres** that all unmistakably belong to **one world**. The connective tissue stays
recognizable; its *expression* changes through color, imagery, composition, density, texture,
type, movement, and mood.

**Different atmospheres, same DNA.** You'd know they're related across a room. They are not
identical, and they are not strangers.

The weekend's real progression, and the felt register each moment should carry:

- **Friday · Little Saint** — creative, relaxed, convivial, colorful, artful. The exhale of
  arriving in Healdsburg. *Vivid paintings, mismatched furniture, communal tables, music in
  the room, plant-forward, everyone talking.*
- **Ceremony · the church** — the most traditional, restrained, composed moment. Hydrangeas,
  a bolero. Quieter and more still, but still unmistakably *ours*. The DNA, dressed up.
- **Cocktail hour · Madrona garden patio** — whimsical, sculptural, ikebana-like Malva + Moss
  florals. Airy, playful, asymmetric, a little strange in the best way. Negative space and
  gesture. Light.
- **Dinner · the Madrona orange grove** — warm, intimate, abundant, unmistakably Californian.
  Orange trees, foliage, evening light, wine, fruit, a long table, real conversation. Rich
  without banquet formality.
- **Dancing · under the porch** — oldies, warm night, nostalgic, romantic, joyful, loose.
  Bodies moving between indoors and out.
- **After-party · inside the Madrona** — art-covered rooms, espresso martinis, friends DJing
  house. Electric, graphic, mischievous, weird-in-the-right-way, a little sexy, late-night.
  The DNA, staying up too late.

**Critical:** these are **not** literal themed chapters with big labels. Do not build "The
Cocktail Hour Section™." The user should **feel** the atmosphere shift — in temperature, density,
palette weighting, rhythm, mark quality — *before consciously noticing* the design system doing
it. The change should register in the body before the mind.

The arc, roughly, moves the whole site: **airy & bright → composed & still → sculptural & light
→ warm & abundant → loose & nostalgic → graphic & electric.** Morning to midnight. The site can
breathe like a day does.

## 5. Materiality

The website should feel like **printed matter translated into a browser** — not a perfect
digital product.

Reach for the sensory memory of:
ink sitting slightly unevenly on toothy paper · a menu someone kept after an incredible dinner ·
a sun-faded poster · a stack of hotel stationery · a painted sign · fruit on rumpled linen · an
artist's sketch tucked into a book · warm light hitting old plaster.

Rules of feeling:

- **Texture is integral, never a filter on top.** No generic noise overlay on an otherwise
  ordinary site. The grain should feel like it's *in* the paper and *in* the ink — like the
  color was printed, not filled.
- **Cream, not white.** Warm paper is the ground. Sterile white is banned as a base.
- **Imperfect registration is a feature.** Slight misalignment of color layers, overprint,
  ink that doesn't perfectly fill its edge — this is screenprint/riso character, and it's how
  the hand stays visible.
- **Edges can be irregular.** Torn, inked, hand-cut. Not everything is a clean rectangle.
- **Finished, not polished.** Keep enough imperfection that a human is obviously responsible.

## 6. The role of color

The supplied palette is the **source of truth** — but it is a palette of **families and shades**,
not "one blue and one orange." Do **not** reduce it. Do **not** turn it into SaaS brand tokens.
Do **not** write rigid color rules yet.

How color should behave:

- **Emotionally and compositionally**, not systematically. Color is a mood instrument.
- **Different atmospheres favor different parts of the palette** while still belonging to one
  world. Cocktail hour can float in pale blues and cream and air; the orange grove can go deep,
  warm, saturated at dusk; the after-party can go graphic and electric with the most intense
  blue in the set. Different atmosphere, same DNA.
- **Warm cream is the connective ground** that lets the palette read as related across very
  different rooms.
- **Flat, saturated, confident** over gradient-soft. The oranges artwork and the Pastoe poster
  both commit to color — no hedging, no glow.
- Blue and orange are the spine, but **the tension between them is the point** — the strange,
  slightly electric adjacency, not a safe complementary balance.

We will define actual values in the SYSTEM phase, sampled from the palette and the Save the Date.
Not now.

## 7. Illustration & mark-making

Illustration is a first-class citizen here, not an accent. The Save the Date proves the idiom:
place, architecture, hand-drawn line, orange, blue, cream, joy, visible human character.

Principles (from the references, as *principles* — never assets to copy):

- **Screenprint / risograph character** — layered flat inks, overprint, limited-color logic.
- **Imperfect, expressive linework** — bent, gestural, confident-not-precise. A hand that knows
  what it's doing and isn't fussing.
- **Drawn marks that carry meaning** — a scribble, a hatch, a stamp, an underline can do real
  work.
- **Graphic and painterly at once** — the oranges artwork is the tone: confident, imperfect,
  *not cute*. Bold enough to be a poster.
- **Authored strangeness** — the Pastoe poster: simple, bold, European, highly authored, a
  little odd. Wit through reduction.

Illustration should feel commissioned by the same people who commissioned the Save the Date —
not literally the same drawing.

---

## 8. Organic form

"Organic-y" here explicitly does **not** mean boho, rustic, earth-tone branding, or generic
botanical decoration. Ban those readings outright.

It means forms that feel **grown, drawn, bent, sculptural, asymmetric, alive, slightly
unpredictable.**

Our teacher is the **Malva + Moss** floral philosophy: *composition, not arrangement.*
Sculptural florals that blur nature and architecture — intentional, restrained, quietly
commanding, ikebana-informed. What we take from it:

- **Gesture and line** over mass and fill.
- **Negative space** as an active material, not leftover emptiness.
- **Interesting silhouette** — the shape between things matters as much as the things.
- **Unexpected relationships between forms** — asymmetric balance, a single stem doing more
  than a full bouquet.
- **Felt, not just seen.**

Organic form shows up in layout, in illustration, in how elements relate on the page — not as
leaves sprinkled in the corners.

## 9. Typography

Typography must feel **collected and culturally intelligent** — chosen by someone with a real
library, not pulled from a wedding-template dropdown.

- There **can** be expressive / handwritten type connected in spirit to the Save the Date —
  but balanced by enough restraint that the site never tips into cute.
- Type should carry **personality, movement, and atmosphere** — it is an active voice, not a
  neutral delivery system for information.
- **Bold scale is allowed and encouraged** — poster logic. Big, confident, a little wild.
- Different atmospheres can shift the typographic register (looser and warmer at dinner, more
  composed at the ceremony, graphic and punchy at the after-party) while staying the same DNA.

**Avoid the default premium-wedding stack:** high-contrast luxury serif + neutral sans + tiny
tracked-out uppercase labels. That trio is the sound of anonymous good taste. We want a voice.

Specific faces come in SYSTEM. Not now.

## 10. Photography

Nirav Patel's sensibility informs the eventual photo treatment: **fine-art documentary,
cinematic, master of natural + artificial light, editorial and observational, reverent toward
quiet human connection, unobtrusive.** Human, warm, intimate, connected to place.

How photography should behave on the site:

- As **editorial material**, not catalog product. It can bleed, crop hard, sit large, sit tiny,
  overlap type, run to an edge.
- **Not** defaulted into identical rounded rectangles or a clean uniform grid. That flattens
  Nirav's eye into a stock library.
- Cropping is a compositional decision every time — unusual, confident, sometimes uncomfortable
  in a good way.
- Light and place first; people are the subject, but the room and the light are why it's *his*.

(We will have real imagery later; in the meantime illustration and composition carry the world.)

## 11. Composition

**Do not begin from conventional website architecture.** Do not reflexively produce:
navbar → centered hero → CTA → three cards → photo strip → FAQ → footer. That is an AI landing
page wearing wedding colors, and it is explicitly the thing to avoid.

Begin instead from **printed matter and ephemera**: posters · menus · matchbooks · invitations ·
artist books · old hotel stationery · restaurant ephemera · magazine spreads · exhibition
catalogs · things pinned to a studio wall.

Compositional tools we *want*:
**scale changes · asymmetry · overlap · cropping · negative space · dense moments next to quiet
moments · irregular edges · unexpected visual rhythm.**

- **Not every piece of information needs a container.** Type can live directly on paper.
- Let some spreads be **loud and dense**, others nearly **empty and quiet**. The rhythm between
  them is the composition.
- Asymmetry and off-center are the resting state. Centering is a deliberate, rare choice
  (the ceremony can earn it).

## 12. Digital interaction

This must be an **amazing website**, not beautiful stationery pasted into a browser. The digital
medium should do things print cannot — and every interaction must have a **point of view**.

The spirit (examples, **not** a checklist — invent better):
illustrations responding to movement · objects shifting or revealing one another · drawn marks
appearing as you go · unusual transitions *between atmospheres* · layered material moving in
space · images changing crop or behavior · typographic play · playful physics · subtle sound
only if truly earned · interactions that make the site feel **discoverable rather than merely
scrollable.**

The bar, again:

> Would someone remember this interaction after closing the site?
> Does it make the wedding world feel more real?

The most promising *directions* (to explore, prove, and kill freely):

- **Transitions between atmospheres** as the signature move — the shift from cocktail-hour air
  to orange-grove warmth should be an authored moment, the thing people remember.
- **The hand appearing** — marks, ink, illustration that draw themselves in as if someone is
  making the page with you.
- **Tactile, physical response** — things that feel like paper, weight, layers, and light
  rather than UI.
- **Discovery** — rewards for wandering, a site that behaves more like exploring the Madrona's
  grounds ("find your own special spaces") than reading a brochure top to bottom.

**Banned as lazy:** generic parallax · random floating blobs · glowing gradients · constant
ambient movement · glassmorphism · effects that exist only to prove we can. Movement is a
choice, and stillness is also a choice.

## 13. The real world this wedding inhabits

Study these for *world*, not to copy. Do not imitate their websites or branding.

- **The Madrona, Healdsburg** — 1881 High Victorian / Aesthetic-Movement mansion, interiors by
  Jay Jeffers: eclectic, pattern-rich, whimsical, a real fine-art collection, antiques and curios
  from lifetimes of travel, statement pieces as the norm. White clapboard, wraparound porch,
  bay windows, ornate detailing. Eight acres of gardens, an **orchard and citrus grove you can
  pick from**, a kitchen garden, cut-flower posies. "The Madrona belongs to the guests" — you're
  meant to explore and find your own corners. Bright and airy in summer. *This is the master
  key to the whole tone: collected, patinated, indoor/outdoor, generous, a little eccentric,
  Californian to the core.*
- **Little Saint, Healdsburg** — Ken Fulk–designed 10,000 sq ft plant-based food/music/arts
  space in the former SHED. Vivid paintings, a custom mural, mismatched furniture, communal
  tables, overstuffed sofas — upscale tea room meets cozy farmhouse. A community hub with live
  music and events. *This is the Friday register: creative, convivial, colorful, artful, loose.*
- **Nirav Patel** — see §10. Fine-art documentary; light, restraint, human truth.
- **Malva + Moss** — see §8. Sculptural, ikebana-informed, composition as architecture as
  emotion; "crafted to be felt, not just seen."

The through-line across all four: **serious craft worn lightly.** Real taste, real art, real
history — held with warmth and ease, never preciousness.

## 14. Anti-patterns — the things that would kill it

If any of these appear, we've drifted. Treat as hard stops:

- Generic Napa luxury · East Coast / preppy wealth codes · boho · rustic
- Generic European-summer wedding branding · watercolor florals · overly feminine wedding styling
- Quiet-luxury beige-everything · generic editorial minimalism · conventional wedding templates
- SaaS layouts · excessive rounded rectangles · glassmorphism · generic gradients · decorative blobs
- Excessive symmetry · excessive centering · fake/costume vintage styling
- Default premium-wedding typography (luxury serif + neutral sans + tiny tracked caps)
- Texture as a noise filter bolted onto an ordinary site
- Interaction for its own sake (parallax, floating shapes, constant motion)
- **Anything algorithmically tasteful but anonymous.** This is the deepest failure mode: the
  site could be beautiful and still be *nobody's*. It must be unmistakably *theirs*.

## 15. The quality bar

The reaction we are **not** aiming for:

> "This is a beautiful wedding website."

The reactions we **are** aiming for:

> "Wait, this is so cool."
> "This feels SO them."
> "I want to keep clicking around."
> "Of course their wedding is at The Madrona."

**The site should leave a residue after someone closes the tab.** If it evaporates the moment
it's gone, we aimed too low.
